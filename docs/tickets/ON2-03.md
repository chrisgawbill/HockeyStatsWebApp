# ON2-03 — Contextual card contracts and offers

Delivery rules: [development workflow](../agent-development.md).

**Product Owner checkpoint:** Unplayed-card lifecycle, exhausted/undersized pools, and hidden CPU commitment behavior.

**Status:** ACTIVE — started by the Product Owner 2026-10-28; implemented on
branch `ON2`. Pending: Spotter run and Product Owner playtest.

**Evidence (2026-10-28):** typecheck, tests (66 files, 320 tests), format and
build PASS. `pnpm test:coverage` FAIL from a pre-existing problem: the two
200-game simulations in `ai/cpuBoardAgent.test.ts` time out under coverage
instrumentation, and they time out the same way on the parent commit. NOT RUN:
Spotter, Product Owner playtest.

**Implementation choices for Product Owner review:** basic actions carry no
tags, so the defense perk never boosts them; greyed-card reasons are now "Not
in this battle" and "Not for your side" (the "Only when checking" reason is
gone).

**Decisions (Product Owner, 2026-10-28):**

- Unplayed offered cards: discarded at the end of the round like played cards,
  and they return on the next reshuffle (the current behavior).
- Undersized pools: after reshuffling eligible discards, any empty offer slots
  are padded with a free, weak basic action so every offer shows three choices.
  The basic action is created for that offer only and never enters the draw,
  discard or exhaust piles. It applies to user and CPU alike, and its exact
  value is tuned in playtest. This is a narrow, approved exception to the
  "no new card content" exclusion.
- CPU intent: before the reveal, show one honest label based on the share of
  the round's energy the CPU committed: Conservative (about 25% or less),
  Balanced, Aggressive, or Heavy Commitment (75% or more). Card names and card
  count stay hidden until the reveal. The label is plain text, not a
  presentation redesign.
- Card map (the design's "Hockey meaning" table applied to today's cards).
  The possession side is the puck carrier: the attacker in deke and intercept
  battles, the defender in check battles.

  | Battle | Possession side | Challenging side |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | Deke | Deke, Toe Drag, Protect Puck, Stickhandle | Poke Check, Body Check |
  | Check | Protect Puck, Stickhandle | Poke Check, Body Check |
  | Intercept | Stickhandle, Deke, Protect Puck | Poke Check |

- Basic action: a side-specific pair, both costing 0 energy. The possession
  side gets "Shield the Puck" (2 resistance); the challenging side gets
  "Stay on Him" (2 pressure). Starting values; tuned in playtest.

**PM implementation notes:** Ordinary offers draw three cards (was five).
Padding applies to ordinary battles; shot and faceoff offers keep drawing from
their own pools. Mid-round draw effects (Stickhandle) draw only eligible cards
and are never padded. Intent bands by the CPU's committed share of base energy:
Conservative ≤ 25%, Balanced ≤ 50%, Aggressive < 75%, Heavy Commitment ≥ 75%.

**Goal:** Ensure every battle draws a small hand of cards that make hockey sense
for that battle and participant side.

**Read:** design “Card system”; card types/data, deck, duel, CPU duel policy,
CardView/DuelScreen only as needed, and focused tests.

**Dependencies:** ON2-01.

**Scope:**

- Represent eligibility by battle context and participant side.
- Draw three eligible cards for ordinary battles; retain shot choose-one offers
  and faceoff offers, including the center's fourth option.
- Keep ineligible cards in their piles and safely reshuffle eligible discards.
- Define unplayed-offer handling and undersized eligible pools under the
  approved lifecycle; never duplicate cards or resurrect exhausted cards.
- Apply identical filters, energy, discard, and exhaust rules to user and CPU.
- Reclassify existing cards into valid contexts without expanding the library.
- Keep exact CPU commitments hidden while exposing broad intent.

**Acceptance:**

- No participant receives a contextually impossible card.
- Seeded offers cannot deadlock when eligible draw piles empty.
- User/CPU tests prove symmetric offers and energy accounting.
- Flexible cards used in one context follow normal discard/reshuffle rules.
- Shot and faceoff timing regressions pass.

**Exclusions:** new card content, team packages, signature cards, presentation
redesign.

**Verify:** card/deck/duel/shot/faceoff/CPU tests, full board-game tests,
typecheck, build.
